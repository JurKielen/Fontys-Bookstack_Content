How a brushed DC motor works

In a brushed DC motor, the commutator acts as a rotating mechanical switch. As the armature turns, the commutator segments slide past the stationary carbon brushes, which connect each coil to the supply at the correct moment and reverse its current direction as needed. This keeps the magnetic field of the armature at a fixed angle to the field of the permanent magnets, so the torque always acts in the same direction and the rotor can complete continuous rotations.

The cross-section shows how the polarity of the armature coils changes during rotation. The middle panel shows the commutator and brushes, and the switching pattern shows which segment each brush touches, both as equivalent switches and over one full revolution.

Try it: change the duty cycle and watch the speed follow it. Turn on slow motion to see a brush bridge two segments at each changeover, which briefly short-circuits one coil.

A BLDC motor has permanent magnets on the rotor and three windings (phases A, B and C) on the stator. The driver energises two of the three phases at a time, which creates a stator magnetic field that the rotor tries to align with. Hall sensors tell the driver where the rotor is, so every 60 electrical degrees it switches to the next of six states and keeps the stator field 60–120° ahead of the rotor. This electronic switching is commutation: the job the brushes and commutator do in a brushed DC motor.

Try it: switch Hall commutation off and press Next state (the rotor just aligns and stops), then switch it on and change the duty cycle.

Motor cross-section

Tint and / badge on a tooth = pole made by its coil (pale = half current, 0 = shorted). ⊙/⊗ = current out of/into the page. Labels 1–2 etc. = the two commutator segments the coil is wired to. Green arrow = armature field.

Commutator & brushes

End view, on the same shaft as the armature. The segments turn, the brushes stay put. A faded segment touches no brush (floating). The PWM switch sets the average voltage: Vavg = D·Vsupply.

Commutator switching pattern

Equivalent switches, not wired to the motor: they only show which segment each brush touches. Two ON in one row = a brush is bridging two segments.

Pattern over one revolution

Green bar above the line = segment on the + brush, bar below = on the − brush, nothing = floating. The red line is the rotor angle; the narrow overlaps are the changeovers.

Inverter (driver switches)

The + phase high-side switch is PWM'd, the − phase low-side switch stays on, the third phase floats. A, B and C go to the motor phases. The duty cycle sets the average voltage: ea = D·Vbus.
