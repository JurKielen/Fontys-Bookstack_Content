How a BLDC motor works

A BLDC motor has permanent magnets on the rotor and three windings (phases A, B and C) on the stator. The driver energises two of the three phases at a time, which creates a stator magnetic field, and the rotor tries to align with it. Hall sensors tell the driver where the rotor is, so every 60 electrical degrees it switches to the next of six states and keeps the field about 90° ahead of the rotor. That switching is commutation, the job the brushes do in a brushed DC motor.

Try it: switch Hall commutation off and press Next state (the rotor just aligns and stops), then switch it on and change the duty cycle.

Driver switches

The + phase high-side switch is PWM'd, the − phase low-side switch stays on, the third phase floats. The duty cycle sets the average voltage: ea = D·Vbus.
